On making room

Reading does not always need a plan. Sometimes it begins with an open book, a comfortable corner, and permission to stay with one thought.

IN THE MARGINS
Attention

The useful pause

Finishing a chapter is not the only way to make progress. A sentence may deserve a second reading, or a moment away from the page. The pause lets a thought become something more than words passing by.

Memory

Returning to a book

A familiar book rarely feels quite the same twice. The pages may be unchanged, but we bring different questions to them. Rereading is a conversation between what we remember and what we notice now.

Habit

A shelf with room

A collection does not need to be complete. A few books within reach can be more inviting than a careful list of everything still to read. Leave a space for an unexpected choice and a book borrowed from a friend.

¶

Write down the question.

When a passage catches your attention, try noting the question it leaves behind instead of summarising it. A question is something you can return to. It keeps the conversation open after the book is closed.

About this small room

Reading at your own pace.

Margins is a quiet collection of reflections on books and the spaces around them. There are no reading targets, ratings, or essential lists here. The idea is simpler: to make a little room for an unhurried page.

There is always another way to begin.
